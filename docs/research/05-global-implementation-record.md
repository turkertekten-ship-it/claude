# What firms actually built, and what happened

> Delegated research, recorded verbatim. This is a subagent's report:
> second-hand under the doctrine in CLAUDE.md, and not promoted to
> established fact anywhere in this repository. The agent reported that
> page fetching was blocked in its environment, so its findings rest on
> search summaries. Confidence grades are the agent's own. Verify anything
> load-bearing against the primary source before acting on it.

**Headline.** The industry's canonical AI success — EQT's Motherbrain, ten years old with a team that grew from 3 to ~20 engineers scanning 50+ data sources — attributes roughly 15 investments to itself in total, which means AI deal sourcing does not pay for itself anywhere near WAM Portfoy's scale; everything with a documented outcome at small scale is document-shaped, narrow-question, and bought rather than built.

## Findings

### 1. EQT Motherbrain's own attributed output is about 15 investments across roughly nine years — the arithmetic, not the narrative, is the decision-relevant fact

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://eqtgroup.com/about/motherbrain)

Motherbrain was founded 2016. Team grew from 3 data scientists to ~20 including full-stack developers, systems engineers and product designers, distributed across North America, Europe and APAC. Publicly cited attribution: 15 investments sourced (one secondary source says 'at least nine start-ups'). That is roughly 1.5-2 attributable deals per year at a firm whose flagship EQT X closed at ~EUR 22bn. Cost per attributable deal is only defensible at that AUM. Scaled to a sub-25-person manager with no engineers, expected yield rounds to zero. Note this is EQT's own number and I could not fetch eqtgroup.com directly (egress blocked) — it comes via search synthesis of EQT pages and secondary coverage.

### 2. What Motherbrain actually ingests is closer to a CRM-plus-thought-record than a predictive model, and its ranking is deliberately interpretable

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://eqtgroup.com/about/digitalization)

Scans 50+ external data sources (including social/web signals) and cross-runs them against internal company data and EQT's own historical due-diligence records. Deal teams log in and see opportunities stack-ranked by relevance and 'momentum score', with explicit emphasis that dealmakers can see WHY something is ranked — interpretable scoring, not a black box. Extends to market analysis, talent acquisition, competitor benchmarking, and (2025-26) 'deep agentic assessments' that combine all public data on a target with internal records. The transferable design point: the moat is the internal record of the firm's own thinking, not the external data, which anyone can buy.

### 3. Bridgewater's AI fund reaches parity with its human flagship, not superiority — after ~2.5 years the honest verdict is 'matches', which is the most under-reported number in the field

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.bridgewater.com/aia-labs)

AIA Macro launched end-2023 at ~USD 2bn, now ~USD 4.5bn AUM. Returns: 11.9% in 2025 (first full year); annualized 11.3% since inception; +8.1% in H1 2026. Bridgewater's human-run flagship macro fund also returned 8.1% in H1 2026 — the AI strategy 'matched pace'. Read plainly: the best-capitalised, longest-running dedicated AI investment effort in macro has produced returns indistinguishable from its own human process. That is a real result (it did not blow up, it scaled 2.25x in AUM) but it is not evidence of AI edge.

### 4. Blackstone's 50+ data scientists are pointed at portfolio-company operations and diligence data, not at deal sourcing — the largest data science group in PE deliberately does not do the thing most vendors sell

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.institutionalinvestor.com/article/2c5n7hypxbs38zj9xsutc/portfolio/how-blackstone-sprinted-ahead-of-its-peers-in-ai)

Team established ~2015, now 50+ data scientists working alongside deal teams worldwide. Documented outcomes cited are operational and portfolio-level: Liftoff +10% revenue post-implementation; Bumble UX/value-proposition work; DNAnexus trial acceleration; Ontra's Synapse engine upgraded with GPT-4 with Blackstone Data Science support. Separately a USD 300m strategic investment in DDN (2025, USD 5bn valuation) — that is AI as an investment theme, a different thing again. No published claim that the group sources deals or that it improved IRR.

### 5. Vista's headline AI numbers are portfolio-company PRODUCT revenue, not investment-process AI — the two get conflated in GP marketing and should be separated before any of it is used as a precedent

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.vistaequitypartners.com/insights/ai-impact-vista-portfolio-2026-mid-year-report/)

Vista Mid-Year 2026 report: more than half of Vista companies monetizing AI products/features; 30 companies with agents in-market; an internal 'Agentic AI factory' used by roughly a third of ~100 portfolio companies. Flagship metric: Nexthink AI ARR grew USD 20m (Q1 2025) to USD 109m (Q1 2026), ~80% IT-ticket resolution rate, projected >USD 200m by early 2027. These are software companies selling AI to their customers. Nothing here is evidence that AI improved Vista's screening, diligence, valuation or monitoring. Meanwhile Thoma Bravo (>USD 181bn, 75+ portfolio companies) and Vista were both publicly reassuring LPs in Feb 2026 that AI is not destroying their software holdings — the same technology is simultaneously the value story and the risk story.

### 6. For KKR, Thoma Bravo, Apollo, Carlyle, Balderton and SignalFire I found no documented internal implementation with a measured investment-process outcome — this absence is itself a finding

*Confidence: low* · [source](https://www.imd.org/entity-profile/kkr-ai-maturity-2025/)

KKR: created an 'infrastructure, data and AI platform organization' inside its technology team in 2025 under CIO Ruchir Swarup; everything else public is external AI infrastructure investing (Helix Digital Infrastructure, USD 10bn+, with NVIDIA, Vistra and the Kuwait Investment Authority, led by ex-AWS CEO Adam Selipsky). No internal outcome disclosed. Thoma Bravo, Apollo, Carlyle: no implementation record with outcomes surfaced. Balderton: no documented platform with outcomes surfaced. SignalFire markets its 'Beacon' platform as tracking population-scale professional and company data, but no independent evaluation of its effect on returns exists. Verdict for all six: unproven. Caveat: my search budget ran out before I could run firm-specific queries for Apollo, Carlyle, Thoma Bravo, Balderton and SignalFire, so this is 'not found in the searches I ran', not an exhaustive negative.

### 7. Hone Capital is the field's cleanest cautionary tale: the celebrated result was an INPUT metric, and the firm subsequently unravelled

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/24/hone-capital-a-silicon-valley-firm-is-being-probed-by-the-fbi/)

McKinsey's 2017 case study: Hone Capital (the Palo Alto arm of China's CSC Group, led by Veronica Wu from 2015) built a model with AngelList, Crunchbase, Mattermark and PitchBook data over ~30,000 deals, testing ~400 characteristics and isolating ~20 predictive of seed success; USD 400m committed to AngelList-sourced deals. The famous number — ~40% of model-selected seed deals raised a Series A versus ~16% industry base rate — is a follow-on rate, an input metric, not a realised return. Subsequent record: CSC Upshot Fund I is closed; TechCrunch reported in September 2024 that the FBI was probing whether Hone's capital came from the Chinese government and whether startup data was shared with Beijing; the firm has been in litigation with its former CFO and former Silicon Valley head since 2020 alleging fraud. No published realised return ever validated the model. Lesson: pick an outcome metric with a denominator, and never cite an interim conversion rate as proof a model works.

### 8. InReach Ventures' '75% of investments sourced by the platform' is self-reported and its investment pace has collapsed

*Confidence: low* · [source](https://medium.com/entrepreneurship-at-work/inreach-ventures-embracing-the-next-chapter-5f811a9513f9)

InReach (Roberto Bonanzinga, London, founded 2015) built the proprietary 'DIG' platform to source European early-stage deals, and claims 75% of all its investments came through it. But the deployment record: 40+ portfolio companies cumulatively, and only 1 investment in 2025, with 1 in January 2026 (NetBird). A Medium post titled 'InReach Ventures: Embracing the Next Chapter' exists — I could not read it (medium.com is egress-blocked here) and cannot say whether it announces a pivot, a wind-down or a new fund. Treat the 75% figure as a marketing claim about attribution methodology the firm defines itself; treat the pace as the observable fact. Verdict: mixed, trending negative, needs verification.

### 9. Correlation Ventures is the single best proof that a SMALL firm can run a model-driven investment process — and its design shows exactly why it works: one narrow question, structured data, a days-long decision loop

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.rebelfund.vc/blog-posts/signalfire-beacon-vs-rebel-fund-vs-correlation-ventures-ai-vc-comparison-2025)

~USD 500m AUM, 200+ portfolio companies (337 companies invested over 16 years), 8 IPOs and 10+ major acquisitions. Predictive model built on tens of thousands of historical VC financings. Critically, the model answers ONE narrow question — should we co-invest on terms a lead investor has already set? — and answers it in days to two weeks. Correlation never leads, never takes board seats. That is the shape a small firm can actually sustain: the question is bounded, the input data is structured and already exists, the decision has a hard deadline, and the model does not have to be right about valuation or strategy. Warning signal: pace has slowed to 10 new investments in 2025 against a 16-per-year average over the prior decade.

### 10. Man AHL has traded machine learning in live client portfolios since early 2014 — twelve years — and the documented value sits in execution cost and risk management, not headline alpha

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.man.com/insights/the-rise-of-machine-learning)

Man AHL has run ML-based systems in its multi-strategy client portfolio since early 2014, with a research relationship through the Oxford-Man Institute, and describes itself as an early adopter of generative AI. Where value is actually documented in the asset-management ML literature: enhancing asset predictors, improving risk management, and driving down execution costs — AHL specifically launched an ML algorithm for trade execution routing. No 2025-26 allocation percentage or ML-attributable return figure was found. The pattern that matters for a non-quant firm: the longest-running successful ML programme in asset management earns its keep on cost and control, in the plumbing, not on prediction.

### 11. The best-documented AI governance failure at an investment firm is Two Sigma's — and it was a model CHANGE-CONTROL failure, not a model-accuracy failure

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.sec.gov/)

An employee made unauthorized changes to live trading model parameters across roughly a dozen models over an extended period, going undetected; the resulting unintended performance impact ran to several hundred million dollars across affected funds. The SEC settled charges against Two Sigma Investments LP and Two Sigma Advisers LP in January 2025, with a combined civil penalty of approximately USD 90m plus reimbursement to affected funds of roughly USD 165m. Figures approximate — I could not re-verify against sec.gov this session (egress blocked). The generalisable point maps directly onto SR 11-7's third pillar: a firm with world-class model DEVELOPMENT had inadequate model GOVERNANCE. For a small firm the minimum viable version of SR 11-7 is cheap and non-negotiable: a model inventory (what runs, who owns it, what it decides), validation independent of the builder, and change control with a frozen evaluation set re-run on every model or prompt change.

### 12. Commercial, purpose-built legal RAG tools marketed as 'hallucination-free' hallucinated 17-33% of the time in independent testing — citations alone are not a control; verified citations are

*Confidence: high* · [source](https://law.stanford.edu/)

Stanford RegLab / HAI study (Magesh, Surani, Dahl, Suzgun, Manning, Ho), 'Hallucination-Free? Assessing the Reliability of Leading AI Legal Research Tools', 2024, later in the Journal of Empirical Legal Studies. Findings on the leading commercial products: Lexis+ AI hallucinated ~17% of the time; Thomson Reuters Westlaw AI-Assisted Research ~33%; Ask Practical Law AI produced incomplete answers or refused at a much higher rate. These are retrieval-augmented systems built by the two dominant legal-data vendors specifically for citation-grounded research — the exact architecture a diligence assistant would use. The design consequence: a link next to a sentence proves nothing. The control is span-level grounding — programmatically checking that the cited passage actually contains the asserted fact, and blocking or flagging the output when it does not. This is the single most transferable technical finding for a regulated document-extraction system.

### 13. Zillow Offers remains the clearest documented case of what happens when a model computes a number that drives capital deployment

*Confidence: high* · [source](https://www.sec.gov/)

Zillow wound down its algorithmic home-buying unit in November 2021 after the pricing model's forecast errors led to systematic overpayment; the company took roughly USD 881m in inventory write-downs and losses and cut about 25% of its workforce. Not an investment firm in the PE/VC sense, but structurally identical to the risk in fund valuation: an ML model produced a valuation, the valuation triggered capital deployment, and errors compounded faster than the human review loop could catch them. This is the empirical backing for the rule that a language model or ML model must never produce a number that lands in a NAV, a valuation, or an LP report — it may extract a number with provenance, and a deterministic engine computes.

### 14. The two large-scale LLM deployments in finance with real adoption both use the same architecture: a curated, vetted internal corpus, mandatory citations, and a named human accountable for the output

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.morganstanley.com/)

Morgan Stanley: 'AI @ Morgan Stanley Assistant' (GPT-4, launched September 2023) answers only from the firm's vetted internal research library of roughly 100,000 documents, not the open web; reported adoption reached ~98% of adviser teams. The follow-on tool, Debrief, generates meeting notes and was reported to save advisers around 30 minutes per client meeting. JPMorgan's LLM Suite reached roughly 60,000 employees during 2024 and was extended firm-wide (~200,000) thereafter. Both are retrieval over owned content with the human retaining responsibility — neither is an autonomous decision-maker. Figures from memory; I could not re-verify this session. The architecture is the finding: closed corpus, cited answers, human sign-off. That is precisely what a regulated small manager can copy at negligible cost.

### 15. The MIT 2026 finding in the existing evidence base has a second half that is more actionable than the 95% headline: shadow AI outperforms sanctioned deployments

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://mlq.ai/media/quarterly_decks/v0.1_State_of_AI_in_Business_2025_Report.pdf)

The same MIT NANDA work that produced the ~95%-of-pilots-fail figure also reported that roughly 90% of employees regularly use personal AI tools for work while only about 40% of firms have official subscriptions — and that the unofficial 'shadow AI economy' frequently produced more real value than the sanctioned enterprise deployments. Read together with the vendor-beats-internal-build finding (external tools succeeded roughly twice as often), the implication for a lean firm is specific: the highest-yield first move is not a build, it is sanctioning, securing and instrumenting what people already do — which converts an uncontrolled MNPI and KVKK exposure into a governed capability at the same time. Extends rather than repeats the base.

### 16. The EU AI Act probably does not reach WAM Portfoy at all, and gold-plating against it would be a self-inflicted cost

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/)

Timeline: in force 1 August 2024; prohibited practices and the Article 4 AI-literacy duty applied from 2 February 2025; GPAI model obligations from 2 August 2025; Annex III high-risk obligations from 2 August 2026; Article 6(1) embedded-product high-risk from 2 August 2027. Crucially, Annex III's financial-services entries are creditworthiness assessment of natural persons (5(b)) and life/health insurance risk pricing (5(c)). Portfolio management and investment decision-making for qualified institutional investors are NOT listed — so a diligence or document-extraction copilot at a GSYF/GYF manager is not high-risk even for an EU firm. Extraterritorial reach under Article 2(1)(c) bites only where the system's output is used in the Union; a Turkish manager serving Turkish qualified investors is largely outside scope. Additional uncertainty: the Commission's November 2025 'Digital Omnibus' proposed delaying the Annex III high-risk date (to around December 2027) pending harmonised standards — I could not verify whether that was adopted before the 2 August 2026 date passed. Verify current status before relying on either reading.

### 17. His binding constraints are KVKK cross-border transfer and the SPK information-systems tebligs — not any AI-specific regulation

*Confidence: medium* · [source](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/)

KVKK (Law 6698) as amended effective 1 June 2024 restructured cross-border personal data transfers around standard contractual clauses, binding corporate rules and narrow derogations, with SCC-based transfers requiring notification to the Kurum within a short deadline (reported as 5 business days). Sending LP records, portfolio-company personnel data or founder data in a prompt to a US-hosted model is a cross-border transfer of personal data and needs a lawful basis and mechanism — this, not the EU AI Act, is the rule that determines the architecture. Separately, SPK's information-systems management communique (VII-128.9) and independent IS audit communique (III-62.2) impose IT governance, data classification, outsourcing/service-provider controls and periodic independent audit on capital markets institutions including portfolio management companies, subject to size thresholds. Any LLM vendor is an outsourced information-system service provider under that framework. Turkey has no AI statute in force as far as I can establish; a draft AI law was tabled in the TBMM in 2024 and I have no evidence it was enacted. All of this needs local counsel verification — I could not reach mevzuat sources (egress blocked).

### 18. Overstating AI capability to investors is itself an enforcement category now, which sets the discipline for how any of this appears in LP materials

*Confidence: high* · [source](https://www.sec.gov/news/press-release/2024-36)

SEC, 18 March 2024: charged Delphia (USA) Inc. and Global Predictions Inc. with making false and misleading statements about their use of AI — 'AI washing'. Combined civil penalties of USD 400,000 (USD 225,000 and USD 175,000 respectively). Neither firm was accused of a bad model; they were accused of describing capabilities they did not have. Related 2024 action against Ilit Raz of Joonko involved AI-related fraud claims to investors. The SPK has no equivalent published action I am aware of, but the practical exposure is identical and closer to home: LP due-diligence questionnaires from 2024 onward routinely ask what AI is used, on what data, with what human oversight, and a mismatch between the DDQ answer and the actual system is a misrepresentation to investors regardless of jurisdiction. Design consequence: whatever is built must be describable in one truthful paragraph, and the audit trail must be able to substantiate it.

## Implications for a small Istanbul GSYF/GYF manager

- Do not build deal sourcing, and now you have the arithmetic rather than just the S&P survey: EQT spent ten years and grew from 3 to ~20 engineers scanning 50+ data sources to attribute ~15 investments. At WAM Portfoy's scale that is a rounding error against a single analyst's referral network. The 64%-ineffective survey number and the EQT number say the same thing from opposite ends of the resource spectrum.
- The one Motherbrain-shaped asset you already own is the Pitch Challenge record — 498 pitches, 395 unique startups, 9 events, Mar-2022 to Sep-2025, with named partners and follow-on state. It is proprietary, longitudinal and labelled, which is more than most emerging managers have. But be precise about what it can do: Hone Capital needed ~30,000 deals to isolate ~20 predictive features. 395 companies will not train a predictor and any model you fit to it will be noise. Use it as a retrieval corpus and a structured CRM — 'what did we see in this space, who pitched it, who was in the room, what happened since' — which is exactly what EQT says Motherbrain actually is.
- Same logic for the LP pipeline tracker (Yildiz, Anadolu, Tekfen, Arcelik, Eko Group with named contacts and follow-up state). Relationship intelligence over your own email, calendar and meeting notes is the highest-yield small-firm application because the data already exists, it is genuinely proprietary, and the job is retrieval and recall rather than prediction. This is the Morgan Stanley architecture at 1/10,000th the scale: closed corpus, cited answers, you remain accountable.
- Citations are not a control; verified citations are. Lexis+ AI hallucinated ~17% and Westlaw AI-Assisted Research ~33% in Stanford's testing — purpose-built, vendor-engineered legal RAG. Any extraction step in your system needs span-level grounding: check programmatically that the cited passage contains the asserted value, and fail closed when it does not. This is the design decision most likely to determine whether the system is usable in an audit.
- Keep the numeric core deterministic and put a reconciliation gate between extraction and computation. The SPK decision of 23 July 2026 is the perfect test case: valuing an exchange-traded GYF/GSYF unit at the founder's last announced unit value is a lookup against a named source with a date, never a generated number. The same rule protects you on TMS 29 restatement. Zillow Offers wrote off ~USD 881m learning what happens when a model's number drives capital deployment.
- Your binding regulatory constraint is KVKK cross-border transfer plus the SPK information-systems communiques (VII-128.9 governance, III-62.2 independent IS audit), not the EU AI Act — which on my reading does not reach a Turkey-only manager serving Turkish qualified investors at all, since Annex III's financial entries cover consumer creditworthiness and insurance pricing, not institutional portfolio management. Decide this explicitly with local counsel rather than defaulting to EU-style compliance you do not owe. Treat any LLM vendor as an outsourced information-system service provider under the SPK framework and paper it accordingly.
- Adopt a minimum viable SR 11-7 on day one — it costs a spreadsheet and a test file. Two Sigma had elite model development and still paid roughly USD 90m in a January 2025 SEC settlement because model CHANGE CONTROL failed. Your version: a model inventory (what runs, who owns it, what it decides, what it must never decide), a frozen evaluation set of questions with known answers re-run on every model or prompt change, and validation by someone who did not build it. With your audit background this is the part you can do better than a firm ten times your size.
- Buy where a vendor exists, build only the thin Turkish-specific layer. MIT's 2:1 vendor-versus-internal-build finding is the strongest evidence available for your headcount, and the exception is narrow and real: no vendor sells SPK obligation tracking with TMS 29 restatement in Turkish. Everything else — fund accounting, document storage, relationship intelligence, company data — is a purchase, and 'building' should mean orchestrating bought components, not training anything.
- Sanction and instrument what your team already does before building anything new. MIT found ~90% of employees using personal AI tools against ~40% of firms with official subscriptions, with the unofficial usage often producing more value. In your case that shadow usage is simultaneously an unmanaged MNPI channel and an unlawful KVKK cross-border transfer. Bringing it inside a governed, logged, zero-retention arrangement is a compliance fix and a capability win in the same move, and it precedes any development work.
- Pick outcome metrics with denominators and refuse input metrics. Hone Capital's famous 40%-versus-16% follow-on rate is an input metric that never converted into a published return, and the firm is now a closed fund under FBI scrutiny. Bridgewater's AIA is the honest benchmark to hold yourself to: after ~2.5 years and USD 4.5bn it MATCHES the human process (+8.1% versus +8.1% in H1 2026). Whatever you build, decide now what number would tell you to switch it off, and be careful how any of this is described to LPs — the SEC fined Delphia and Global Predictions USD 400,000 combined in March 2024 purely for describing AI they did not have.
