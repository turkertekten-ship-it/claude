02 / EVIDENCE S&P 2026 · MIT 2026

The industry rates AI ineffective at exactly what it is sold for

This is not a hedge. Managers were asked, and the two use cases most heavily marketed to a firm like yours are the two they rank lowest.

64%Rate AI ineffective for deal sourcing
75%Rate it ineffective for portfolio monitoring

The arithmetic from the canonical success is worse than the survey. EQT's Motherbrain — the case every vendor cites — took ten years and roughly twenty engineers to attribute about fifteen investments. At your scale that is a rounding error against one partner's phone.

What does work is narrow and unglamorous: document-heavy extraction, and a deterministic numerical core that a model never touches. That is the whole design.

The strongest evidence available argues against building anything at all.

External vendor tools succeed roughly twice as often as internal builds. That finding is recorded rather than buried, and it wins nearly everywhere — fund accounting, custody, document storage and audit should all be bought. It loses in exactly one place, for a reason specific to Turkey.

03 / THE PIVOT AIR-4 · unconfirmed

A residency rule flips build-versus-buy

Tebliğ VII-128.10 requires a capital-markets institution to keep both its primary and its secondary information systems inside Turkey. If that binds a portföy yönetim şirketi, the US-hosted fund-ops stack — Carta, Juniper Square, Affinity — cannot be your system of record, and the vendor evidence does not transfer, because the vendors are not deployable.

Add that no vendor sells SPK obligation tracking with TÜFE restatement in Turkish, and the buildable surface narrows to something one numerate person can own.

This is the load-bearing assumption of the entire design, and it is not settled. It rests on legal commentary, not the SPK's own text — the regulator's site was unreachable from where this ran. If counsel reads VII-128.10 and says it binds narrowly or not at all, a meaningful part of this should be replaced by a purchase order. It is the first question to resolve.

04 / SYSTEM SPK 11/255

What it is, and what it refuses

It watches the obligations and the numbers that keep four fund licences clean, and makes every figure it emits traceable to its source and honest about inflation. A control system, not an investment system.

It deliberately does not

  • Source deals. The evidence and the EQT arithmetic agree from opposite ends of the resource spectrum.
  • Render a monitoring dashboard — the thing that gets demoed and stops being opened in week three.
  • Let a language model near a number. Models read documents and draft prose; deterministic code owns money, dates, obligations and NAV.
  • Send anything. Every executor writes to disk. Drafting is what a system can be trusted with; filing is a decision with your name on it.

Fund figures are nominal. Management-company figures are TMS 29 restated. They may never be added.

SPK decision 16.02.2024 no. 11/255 exempts investment funds from inflation accounting while the manager itself applies it — so the two are denominated in different money. At 31.75% CPI, a consolidated view that mixes them is wrong by roughly a third per year of divergence, in the flattering direction. The Money type refuses the addition rather than trusting anyone to remember.

The second invariant

A nominal return is not a return. At 32% inflation a 40% nominal IRR is about 6% real — (1.40 / 1.32) − 1. The naive subtraction gives 8%, overstating by a third of the answer, and the error grows precisely as inflation rises. Every metric has a real_ twin, and the brief always prints both.

05 / DECIDE 17 rules · no model

Rules decide, not a model

Every rule is a Python predicate over typed state, so an auditor can read one and say whether it is right. Each carries a stated reason for its threshold, because after a month the question is never what does this rule do but why does it keep firing. Most are demoted to digest: an alerting system fails by firing until nobody reads it, and then it misses everything.

MODEL-CHANGED is not decoration. Two Sigma settled with the SEC for roughly $90m in January 2025 over a failure of model change control, not model quality. A model inventory, a frozen eval set re-run on every change, and validation by someone who did not build it is a minimum viable SR 11-7 — and it costs a spreadsheet and a test file. With your audit background it is the part you can do better than a firm ten times your size.

REG-DEADLINE-SHORT is calibrated on a live example: the SPK's 23 July 2026 valuation decision gave portfolio managers until 31 July to comply. Eight days. No quarterly review catches that.
